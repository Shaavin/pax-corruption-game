## Resolved

### 2026-11-26 (Joe, via Ian): balance batch and Fund a monument

| Topic | Ruling |
| --- | --- |
| Fund a monument: which cards | Any card from your hand or network (and the Market's Dragonara civils, which State Enterprise lets you use as if in hand) whose **printed district** matches the monument's: the same cards that pay for building it. General Elections never. |
| Fund a monument: tucked cards | **Face up**, public to both players. **One card** per funding. |
| Fund a monument: building it | The tucked cards are discarded **with** the builder's own, to the builder's support piles, and count as the builder's discards: Space Academy may reroute them, and Martial Law counts them (so a Shavvinne monument still can't be built under Martial Law). |
| Architecture Inc. | "Shavvinne cards are considered wild": for that free construction, Shavvinne cards count as cards of the monument's district. The cost is still 5 less funding. They are discarded to the Shavvinne pile, as printed. |
| Capitol Inc. | "You may choose any district policy as target": any district, not only one where a Senator was convinced. A District Governor's "cannot be targeted by policy proposal" still protects its district. |
| Kybernetik Inc. | "Right after main action": take a main action first; then you may draw to your hand limit. If you do, the action step is over, you still get your Politics step, and you skip that turn's Income step. It can no longer replace the main action (supersedes the 2026-11-11 ruling). |
| Rebel Inc. | "Get +2 partisans if you have fewer partisans": a recruit through Rebel Inc. gains **2** partisans if you have fewer than your opponent (before recruiting), else the usual **1**. |
| Urban Aristocrats | "Swap one of your played cards with an opponent's in Dragonara": **any** card for any other, civil or alliance (Urban Aristocrats itself included), as long as the result is legal: nobody may end up with more alliances in Dragonara than their limit. The swapped cards change owners, as before. |
| South events | "<District> cards now convince all <District> Senators": a revealed card of that district, of **any value or kind**, convinces **every** Senator of that district, **conspiracy Senators included**, for either player. Nothing is convinced without a reveal: the old "always convinced" (and proposing with nothing revealed) is gone. |

### 2026-11-11 (Joe, via Ian)

| Topic | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Monument setup | Monuments **may** share a district. Setup: the 2 guaranteed by the parties + 2 random from the rest, with **no** district constraint. Replenishing after construction is random too. |
| Turn order around elections | Normal turn order **never changes**. The election phase is just a state of the Market. Example: if player A reveals a General Election during their income step, player B takes the next turn, which is the first turn of the election phase. After resolution, play keeps alternating. No first-player choice. |
| Roman Basilica | Its owner may declare Civil Victory after winning **3 elections** (3 election phases won, not General Election cards). This is a remnant of the old primary win condition; now only the final election wins Civil for everyone else. Declared like other victories, in the owner's Politics step. They may also still win Civil normally at the final election. **Superseded 2026-11-18:** the owner wins automatically (3rd election featuring the 3rd General Election card, or the end of the final election). |
| Planet Next ("bank") | Owner may move Horsard cards **from hand only** into a personal face-up bank. Banked cards don't count toward hand limit. Each banked card gives its owner **+1 influence in the Horsard electorate**. Banked cards can be used later **as if in hand** for any action (play, Recruit, Construct, Influence Senate, Propose Policy reveals, …). When a card leaves the bank, its +1 goes with it. Being further under the hand limit at income is intended. Clarified 2026-11-11: the bank is an **extension of your hand**. You may bank cards only during your own turn. Banked cards have **no other interactions**: conspiracies, policies, alliances, and decrees ignore them, and they are never discarded except when you spend them. **Superseded 2026-11-18:** the bank became the Horsard network (see Network). |
| General Elections in one phase | A General Election revealed at any time (including mid-turn) **starts the election immediately**; the Market grows to 8 and the turn carries on. Any more revealed during that election are added to it, and the winner activates (chooses) one inactive decree **per General Election** that came up in that election. |
| Century Inc. | Once per turn; counts as playing an alliance (Model Society, Currency Ballots, …). It must **replace** an existing alliance, in your tableau or the opponent's; with no alliance to replace, it can't be used. In the opponent's tableau, their alliance goes to **their** support pile for that district, and your played alliance is then **theirs**, in that district. Replaced cards always go to the support pile of the player whose tableau held them. An alliance naming a district keeps that effect for its **printed** district, but it occupies the lane it's in: a conspiracy there wipes it. The alliance limit is per district, counting whatever alliances sit in that lane regardless of printed district. |
| Discarding events (expansion) | Any event card that would be discarded (conspiracy wipe, legacy event, etc.) goes **back to the box**: removed from the game, never into a support pile. This overrides the expansion rulebook line that sends events played as civil cards to a support pile. |
| Conspiracies vs. events (expansion) | A conspiracy wipes event cards played **as civil cards** (for their number) in its district. Events played for their **effect** (in the common area) are never wiped by conspiracies. |
| Kybernetik Inc. | **Superseded 2026-11-26** (now only after the main action). May be used **before** your main action; using it ends the action step, so it replaces the main action that turn. You still get your **Politics** step; only income is skipped, and that includes the Market refill: if a Market card was taken during the turn (e.g. via State Enterprise), the Market stays short until the next income step that refills it. A General Election drawn this way starts the election as usual. |
| Decree activation | The election winner **chooses** which inactive decree(s) to activate. If more General Elections were set aside than inactive decrees remain, the extras are forgotten. |
| Final election | Every General Election is guaranteed to be drawn before the deck runs out, so the game always reaches a final election unless someone declares a victory first. |
| Influence Senate | **Any** card from hand can be seated. The pushed-out Senator goes to **your** support pile for its district and counts like any other support-pile card (influence, Popularity, Recruit precondition, and so on). |
| Propose Policy matching | Revealed cards only convince Senators of the **same district**. A Horsard civil 6 can convince lower Horsard civil Senators, never Shavvinne, Smarbbit, or Dragonara ones. |
| Election-phase income | The Market doesn't refill during an election, but a player under the hand limit **still draws from the deck**. (Expansion: event cards keep discarding and replenishing by their own rules, including disappearing from the last two spots of their row.) |
| Tie-break cards | Tie-break 2 counts civil cards (both players) in each won district and alliance cards (both players) for a won Council of Lords. |
| De Jures | Compare each district separately. A player with **no** civil cards in a district loses that district's comparison. ~~If **neither** player has civil cards there, it's a tie, which satisfies "tie for lowest" for both.~~ **Superseded 2026-11-17:** an empty district fails for both. Satisfied only if the player passes in all 4 districts. |

### 2026-11-18 (Joe, via Ian): card updates and the network

| Topic | Decision |
| --- | --- |

Disabled by default; an ability enables it for one district, and only that district's cards can go in. During your own turn (action, politics, income) you may move cards in and out freely. Network cards are usable as if in hand. When a network is disabled, its cards go back to the hand, even past the hand limit (no discard). |

| Space Academy (was Space Agency) | "You may discard up to 1 of the cards to another support pile each time you discard." One card per discard in total, to another of your own piles. Innovative Party now names it. The card id stays `space-agency`. |

### 2026-11-16 (Ian)

| Topic | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Market flow (expansion) | Cards flow **left to right like a conveyor**. New cards enter on the left; older cards slide right to close gaps, so a short row sits in its right-most spots. At the income refill: events that started the turn in the last 2 spots leave, the rest slide right, a new event (if fewer than 2 and there's room) takes **spot 1**, and main cards fill the gap. An event therefore starts expensive and gets cheaper as it drifts right, until it leaves. During an election a row without an event gets one on its left only if that isn't one of the last 2 spots (the row holds 2+ cards). When election rows open, a row without an event gets its new event in spot 1, consistent with the post-election ruling. **Superseded 2026-11-21 for the income refill:** the new event goes in first, next to the older cards, and main cards follow to its left. After an election and in new election rows it still takes spot 1. |

### 2026-11-14 (Ian)

| Topic | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| North decree flip vs. activation (expansion) | While a North event flips a decree, the election winner chooses among decrees whose **current** side is inactive. Activating one flips its printed side, so when the event leaves the decree shows the other side again. Confirmed with Joe (2026-11-15). |
| Propose Policy reveals | Every revealed card must convince **at least one** Senator, and **no Senator may be convinced by two revealed cards**. To convince two civil Senators of a district, reveal one higher card that beats both, or a lower card that beats only the lower one, never both cards. In practice: at most one civil and one alliance per district. Edge case: if South events alone already convince a majority, the player may propose **without revealing or discarding any card**. (Ian, 2026-11-15.) |
| Legacy activation when all are active (expansion) | A legacy event can only activate a victory that is still inactive; if all five are active, the only option is "discard played <power> cards". Confirmed with Joe (2026-11-15). |
| Income with only events in the Market (expansion) | The refill never leaves only events, but a turn's own actions can (an East take or State Enterprise, most likely during an election when the Market isn't refilled with main cards). At income: if the player can afford one of those events, they **must** buy it. If they can't, they lose the Market take for that turn but still draw from the deck as usual. (Ian, 2026-11-15.) |
| Buying an event at the hand limit (expansion) | Events are played at once and never sit in the hand, so buying one needs no room. A player at the hand limit can't take a Market card but may buy an affordable event, or pass and take nothing (buying stays optional there). Under the limit, income is unchanged. (Ian, 2026-11-16.) |
| Event-effect draw (expansion house rule) | The extra card is drawn right after the purchase, even if it takes the buyer over the hand limit. The normal end-of-income draw still needs room. Confirmed with Joe (2026-11-15). |
| Market at game start (expansion) | The Market starts with 5 main-deck cards and **no** event; the first event arrives at the first income refill. The setup steps don't place one. Confirmed with Joe (2026-11-15). |
| Events entering mid-turn (expansion) | An event that enters the Market during a turn (election rows formed mid-turn, the income refill) is never removed by the "last 2 spots" check in that same turn. This follows from the confirmed "last 2 spots" rule below: only events that sat in spot 4 or 5 **at the start of the turn** are removed. |
| "Last 2 spots" (expansion) | A row has 5 spots; an event counts as in the last 2 if it sat in spot 4 or 5 of that row at the **start of the turn**. At every income refill, **however many cards the Market holds**, check the last 2 spots, collapse, then replenish, never past 5 cards (a full Market with nothing removed gets no new card, not even an event). Confirmed with Joe (2026-11-15), who corrected the second half: the refill used to be skipped when the Market already had 5 cards. |
| South "influence reduced to support" (expansion) | Influence there is **strictly** the support count: civil values, the Planet Next bank, Order of Knights, Economic Reform, Secularism, and **Populist Forum**'s doubling all drop out. (West's "support counts as zero" still applies first.) Confirmed with Joe (2026-11-15), who added that Populist Forum is ignored too. |
| Party symbol district (expansion, West "disable icons") | A party's ideological symbol belongs to the district whose color it is printed in, which always matches its guaranteed monument's district (Trinity's green crown = Shavvinne). Decrees (printed purple) are never disabled. Confirmed with Joe (2026-11-15). |
| Election rows with 2 events (expansion) | When an election opens with 2 events in the Market, the event that came **later** (the one further **left**) drops into the **same spot** of the new second row. Nothing else moves: the gap it leaves in row 1 and every other open spot fill in place from the main deck. Example: events in spots 2 and 3; the spot-3 event stays in row 1, the spot-2 event goes to spot 2 of row 2. (Ian, 2026-11-15; replaces the reading where the right-most event dropped and row 1 closed up.) |
| Market after an election (expansion) | When an election ends, **nothing** stays in the Market: leftover event cards leave the game (to the box). Then the Market refills like a normal income refill: one new event is revealed into the **first** spot (costing 4 to buy) and main-deck cards fill it to 5. The rulebook line "putting an Event card at the last slot" has this backwards. (Ian, 2026-11-15; replaces the "4 main cards + a new event in the 5th spot" reading.) |
| Propose Policy target | The changed policy must be in a district where the proposal **convinced a Senator**, whichever card (or South event) convinced them. A district with no convinced Senator stays off limits. (Ian 2026-11-14, refined by Joe 2026-11-15: eligibility follows the convinced Senators, not the revealed cards. So a South "always convinced" Senator opens its district even with no matching card revealed. Conspiracy Senators are never convinced, so they never open one.) Applies to Capitol Inc.'s free proposal too. |
| Foreign Influence setup (expansion) | Setup as usual: set the 8 legacy events aside, remove 3 random regular events per power (12), shuffle the remaining 20 with the legacy events into the event deck (28). Then take **3 random events from the 12 removed** as the starting events; the other 9 stay in the box. (Ian, 2026-11-15; corrects the earlier "drawn from the 20 left after removing" interpretation.) |
| Main action required | If a player can reach a main action during their turn (directly, or after free actions such as an East Market take), they **must** take one. Free actions that leave no main action reachable are not allowed before the main action; the engine never offers them, which is the same as "undo the free action". If no main action can be reached at all, the player **draws a card** (even at or over the hand limit) instead, takes **no free actions** that turn, and still plays **Politics and Income** as usual. Setting cards aside (Planet Next, Consultative Office, East reveal) is a special ability, not a free action, and is unaffected. (Kybernetik Inc. used to count as reaching a main action; since 2026-11-26 it comes only after one.) |
| East "Revealed <district> cards do not occupy hand limit" (expansion) | Confirmed with Joe. Works **like Planet Next**: during your own turn (action, politics, or income) the event's buyer may move cards of that district from hand to a face-up area. They are usable as if in hand and don't count toward the hand limit. If the event leaves play (discarded, legacy, …), those cards go **straight back to the hand**, even if that puts the player **over the hand limit**. That is allowed: there is no discard down to the limit. The player simply can't take a Market card or draw at income until the hand is back under the limit. **Superseded 2026-11-18:** these events now read "Enable network in <district>" (see Network); the back-to-hand rule carries over. |
